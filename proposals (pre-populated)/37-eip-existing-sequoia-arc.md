CATEGORY: EIP
TITLE: Add four regional Sequoia Arc nodes so the program stops being location-gated

ABSTRACT: Sequoia Arc runs as a structured pre-seed and seed program in San Francisco and London. The program is high-quality and the alumni outcomes are real, but the location requirement filters out a meaningful portion of the founders the program is intended to serve. The wedge is to add four regional Arc nodes (Bangalore, São Paulo, Tel Aviv, Singapore) running the same curriculum on the same cadence with local programming-chairs and an in-person intensive in SF or London once during the program. The center of gravity of the curriculum stays the same. The geographic on-ramp opens up.

BODY:
Sequoia Arc has the kind of curriculum and partner attention that a `$0 to $3M ARR` founder genuinely cannot get anywhere else. The program operates from two cities. The implicit cost of attending is the cost of relocating one founder for ten to twelve weeks plus the cost of being absent from wherever the company's customers and team actually are. For founders building in or for non-US-and-non-UK markets, this is a tax on the program that biases against the founders the global Arc network most needs.

## What's wrong with the current format

Arc is a structured pre-seed and seed program that runs roughly twice per year per location with cohorts of `~15 to 20` companies. The curriculum is partner-led, the network access is real and the post-program follow-on capital from Sequoia is the natural next step for the strongest cohorts. By the metrics that matter (graduation rate, follow-on funding rate, Series A conversion), the program is among the strongest VC-run accelerators of its generation.

The location-bound design has tradeoffs. The benefits are real (network density in SF and London, partner presence, shared work environment). The costs are less visible but compound. A founder building B2B SaaS for India who relocates to SF for three months loses meaningful customer-engagement time during a stage where customer signal is most valuable. A founder building for the LatAm market who relocates to London ends up in a curriculum optimized for a different market shape. A founder who cannot relocate at all (for family reasons, immigration constraints or capital constraints) self-selects out.

By my read of the cohort composition data Sequoia publishes about Arc, roughly `~60 to 70%` of cohort participants are based in or near the program's host city, and the remaining `~30 to 40%` relocated for the program. The international participation, while real, is dominated by founders who could afford to relocate. The selection bias is not against any specific founder profile in principle but against any founder whose business or life cannot absorb the relocation cost.

Sequoia operates a global network of partners and portfolio companies across at least eight major geographies. The Arc footprint not matching that footprint is a self-imposed constraint with an obvious fix.

## Proposed format

Add four regional Arc nodes with the same program structure, same cadence, same curriculum and one shared in-person intensive.

**New nodes.** Bangalore (covering India and South Asia), São Paulo (covering Brazil and the LatAm corridor), Tel Aviv (covering Israel and select Middle East and Europe), Singapore (covering Southeast Asia and parts of East Asia). The four cities cover the highest-density founder geographies outside SF and London.

**Cohort size per node.** Twelve to fifteen companies. Smaller than the SF or London cohort by design, because the local talent pool in each new city for Sequoia's typical company shape is smaller and the program quality should not be diluted to fill cohort seats.

**Cadence.** Two cohorts per year per node. Six nodes total (SF, London, Bangalore, São Paulo, Tel Aviv, Singapore) running twelve cohorts per year combined, with roughly `~150 to 180 founders` graduating annually.

**Curriculum.** Shared across nodes. The Arc curriculum (product strategy, go-to-market, fundraising readiness, operating cadence) is largely market-agnostic and translates directly. Vertical adaptations (local regulatory considerations, local customer-acquisition channels, local talent market dynamics) added per node by the local partner team.

**In-person intensive.** One two-week in-person intensive during each cohort, held in either SF or London, where founders from all nodes converge. This is the network-density event that the location-bound version of Arc currently provides for free. Founders fly in for two weeks. Sequoia covers the travel.

**Local programming chair.** Each node has a dedicated lead partner who runs the local cohort, with cross-node collaboration on curriculum and selection.

**Host shape.** Sequoia partners at the lead level for each node (the firm has partners in most of these geographies already). Plus operating partners and former founders as session leaders, drawn from each node's local Sequoia network.

**Success criterion.** Twelve months after each cohort, what fraction raised a Series A from any credible fund (Sequoia or other)? Floor: `~45%`. Stretch: `~65%`. The benchmark should match the existing SF and London Arc cohorts within `~10 percentage points`. If the new nodes underperform that, the local programming chair or selection process needs re-examination.

**Kill criterion.** Two consecutive cohorts at a node with Series A conversion below `~25%`. Pause and re-examine the local cohort fit and selection process. If two more cohorts after re-examination still underperform, retire that node.

## Mechanics

- **Application.** Single global application form. Founders specify which node they want to join (typically the one closest to their company's center of gravity). Cross-node assignments allowed when the founder's product or market argues for it.
- **Selection.** Local selection committees at each node, with cross-node review. Final cohort lists approved by the global Arc lead.
- **Capital.** Sequoia's standard Arc check (terms not publicly disclosed but generally aligned with seed-stage Sequoia investments). Same check across all nodes.
- **Local space.** Each new node needs a physical working space for cohort meetings. Sequoia's existing local offices serve some of this. For nodes where Sequoia does not have meaningful office presence, leased coworking space at `~$200K to $400K per year per node`.
- **In-person intensive cost.** Two weeks per cohort, in SF or London. Travel for `~75 to 90 founders` per intensive (combined across cohorts running at the time). Travel and accommodation budget: `~$300K to $500K per intensive`, `~$600K to $1M per year` for two intensives annually.
- **Total incremental annual cost.** Operating expenses for four new nodes plus the intensives: `~$5M to $9M per year`. Significant for a single program. Trivial as a percentage of Sequoia's annual operating budget across the firm.
- **Smallest viable version.** Launch one new node first (Bangalore is the most natural choice given existing Sequoia India presence and India founder density). Run two cohorts. If Series A conversion meets the SF/London benchmark, scale to the additional three nodes.

## Risks

**Risk: cohort quality at new nodes is lower than at established nodes, and the brand of Arc dilutes.** Mitigation: explicit cohort size discipline (smaller is better than padded). Selection committees set quality floors that match SF and London standards even if it means smaller cohorts than the program structure allows.

**Risk: the in-person intensive concentrates value-extraction in SF and London, so the new nodes feel like second-class extensions.** Mitigation: rotate the in-person intensive location every other cycle (some intensives in Bangalore or Tel Aviv, not always in SF or London). The Arc community of all nodes shares the convening, not just the periphery converging on the center.

**Risk: the local programming chair at a new node lacks the network density to deliver value comparable to SF or London.** Mitigation: explicit cross-pollination. SF and London partners spend a week per cycle at each new node. The new-node lead partner spends two weeks per cycle in SF or London. The network density transfers through partner mobility.

**Risk: the program at new nodes becomes a regional accelerator (good but not differentiated from local accelerators) rather than a true Arc extension.** Mitigation: the in-person intensive in SF or London is the structural defense. The Sequoia partner involvement is the experiential defense. The follow-on capital relationship to Sequoia's global fund is the financial defense.

**Risk: travel costs for the intensives are absorbed by the program, but founders' time away from their company during a critical stage is harder to absorb.** Mitigation: the intensive is two weeks, not ten. Founders can manage two weeks. The remaining ten weeks of programming run locally with no travel.

## Open questions

Should the curriculum be globally consistent or adapt per market? My read is roughly `~80% global, ~20% local`. The fundamentals (product strategy, customer development, fundraising motion) translate. The specifics (local regulatory environment, local customer acquisition channels, local talent market shape) require local adaptation. The mix is similar to how multinational law firms structure their training programs.

How does this affect the existing SF and London cohorts? Probably mildly positively. The cross-node network density of the in-person intensives gives SF and London cohorts access to global founders they would not otherwise meet. The program quality at SF and London should not degrade and may improve.

What about second-tier geographic hubs (Sydney, Berlin, Lagos, Toronto, Mumbai)? Probably out of scope for the first generation of the expansion. Add nodes only when the local founder density and Sequoia local presence both support it. The four named cities are the obvious starting set.

How does this interact with Sequoia's separate fund structures (Sequoia India and Southeast Asia operating under different legal entities, for example)? The legal complexity is real and resolvable. The Arc program structure can run across legal entities with capital deployment routed through whichever fund is the appropriate investor for each company. This is operational complexity, not a program-design problem.

When does the program expand beyond four new nodes? Probably not for at least three years from the first expansion. The first wave needs to prove out before the second wave begins. The risk of over-expanding is a program that loses the quality bar that made Arc work in SF and London originally.
