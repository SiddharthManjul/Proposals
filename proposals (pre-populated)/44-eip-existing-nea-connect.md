CATEGORY: EIP
TITLE: Reshape NEA Connect into vertical-track parallel cohorts with shared plenary

ABSTRACT: NEA Connect (the firm's annual portfolio CEO summit) draws CEOs from across NEA's broad multi-stage, multi-sector portfolio. The breadth is part of NEA's identity as one of the largest and most diversified VC platforms, but it works against peer-learning at the summit. The wedge is to restructure Connect into vertical-track parallel cohorts (healthcare, enterprise SaaS, consumer and AI infrastructure, deep tech, fintech) running their own programming alongside a single shared plenary. Each vertical cohort is small enough to function as a peer group. The shared plenary preserves the firm-wide identity event. The summit becomes five summits in one, organized around the way operational learning actually works.

BODY:
NEA is one of the few VC firms whose portfolio plausibly spans every major technology sector at meaningful scale. The breadth has competitive advantages (cross-sector pattern recognition, broad partner network, optionality on emerging categories) and a structural cost (the firm's portfolio cannot function as a single peer cohort because no two adjacent CEOs in the room are typically working on adjacent problems). The annual summit format inherits this cost.

## What's wrong with the current format

NEA Connect runs annually as a multi-day portfolio summit with attendees drawn from NEA's broad portfolio. By my count, the attendee list runs roughly `~300 to 500 portfolio leaders`, distributed across healthcare (a major NEA focus), enterprise SaaS, consumer, AI and deep tech in roughly equal portions. Programming includes shared keynotes, partner-led sessions and breakout tracks.

The structural problem is that "portfolio CEOs at a multi-sector firm" is not a peer cohort. A CEO of a clinical-stage biotech and a CEO of a Series C horizontal SaaS company have almost no overlapping operational concerns. They can have an interesting conversation across the dinner table, but they cannot meaningfully help each other with this quarter's roadmap. The peer-learning value of the summit therefore depends entirely on whether the attendee can find their five or six actual peers among the three hundred attendees. By my read, most CEOs at Connect form one to two genuinely useful peer relationships per summit, which is a real number but below what a properly cohort-matched summit produces (typically four to six per summit).

The breakout tracks at Connect partially address this. Sectoral breakouts allow some same-sector clustering. But the breakouts are typically one or two sessions per day rather than the full summit structure, and the rest of the summit reverts to mixed-cohort programming. The CEO ends up with two hours of high-peer-density programming and twelve hours of mixed-cohort programming over the summit.

The cost of the current structure is the gap between what the summit could deliver (substantial peer cohort experience for every attendee) and what it currently delivers (modest cross-sector network plus thin sector-specific peer experience). The cost is hard to measure directly but visible in the comparison between Connect-attendee outcomes and the outcomes of CEOs who participate in industry-specific summits where the cohort match is naturally tighter.

## Proposed format

Restructure Connect into vertical-track parallel cohorts plus a single shared plenary.

**Five vertical tracks.**

*Healthcare and life sciences.* Biotech, medtech, healthcare delivery, digital health. `~80 to 120 attendees`.

*Enterprise SaaS.* Horizontal and vertical SaaS, B2B software, productivity tools. `~80 to 120 attendees`.

*Consumer and AI.* AI-native products, consumer apps, prosumer tools. `~60 to 100 attendees`.

*Deep tech.* Hardware, semiconductors, robotics, frontier physical-science startups. `~40 to 80 attendees`.

*Fintech.* Payments, lending, vertical fintech, infrastructure for financial services. `~40 to 60 attendees`.

**Track programming.** Each track runs its own two-day program inside the larger Connect umbrella. Track programming is peer-cohort focused: workshops on stage-specific operational topics, structured small-group conversations, partner-led sessions with vertical-relevant operating partners.

**Shared plenary.** One half-day plenary across all tracks at the start of the summit, plus an evening reception. The plenary is the firm-wide identity event: managing partners' annual remarks, portfolio-wide announcements, the brand-cohesion moment. After the plenary, attendees split into their vertical tracks. They reconvene for a closing dinner on the final evening.

**Cross-track interaction.** Limited and structured. One cross-track session per summit, focused on an explicit cross-cutting topic (typically a regulatory or macro topic that affects multiple verticals). Attendees self-select into the cross-track session based on interest. Otherwise tracks run in parallel.

**Cadence.** Annual, unchanged.

**Host shape.** NEA partners distribute across tracks based on their sector focus. A healthcare partner anchors the healthcare track, an enterprise partner anchors the enterprise SaaS track, and so on. The firm's managing partners attend the shared plenary and rotate through tracks during the rest of the summit.

**Off-the-record.** Chatham House throughout. The breadth of NEA's portfolio means that competitors in adjacent companies could plausibly attend the same track. The off-the-record rule and the careful seat assignment by sub-vertical handle this.

**Success criterion.** Cohort cohesion: at six months post-summit, what percentage of attendees report at least three ongoing peer relationships formed at the summit? Floor: `>55%`. Stretch: `>75%`. Plus track attendance retention year over year: floor `~75%`.

**Kill criterion.** Two consecutive summits at a specific track with cohesion below `~35%`. Re-examine that track's selection and programming before retiring the track.

## Mechanics

- **Track assignment.** Each portfolio company assigned to a track based on its primary sector and stage. Companies that straddle tracks (a health-tech SaaS company that could fit healthcare or enterprise SaaS) assigned to whichever track has the closer peer cohort for their sub-vertical.
- **Cross-track participation.** Companies can request cross-track invitations to specific sessions of interest. Limit of `~10 to 15 cross-track invites per summit` per attendee to prevent over-fragmentation.
- **Programming per track.** Track programming designed by the track's lead partner with input from track attendees. Each track has a track-specific operating partner anchor for functional sessions.
- **Venue.** Single destination property hosting all five tracks plus the shared plenary. Venue needs to support five parallel tracks running concurrently, which means at least five medium-sized meeting rooms plus a plenary hall plus dining space. Existing NEA Connect venues may need to upgrade or change.
- **Budget.** Five parallel tracks at smaller per-track scale plus the shared plenary cost more in operational complexity than a single combined summit (`~30 to 50%` more), so total cost runs `~$2.5M to $4M annually` versus the current `~$2M to $3M`. The increase reflects more programming, more operating-partner time, more parallel-event coordination. The per-attendee value should increase by more than the proportional cost.
- **Operating-partner time.** Sector-aligned operating partners spend their summit time within their own track. The aggregate operating-partner-time at the summit increases roughly `~25 to 40%`, from `~10 to 14 weeks` to `~14 to 18 weeks`. Justified by the depth of engagement within each track.
- **Documentation.** Per-track summary distributed to track attendees plus a firm-wide summary of cross-cutting themes. No external publication.
- **Smallest viable version.** Pilot a single vertical-track structure (healthcare is the natural choice given NEA's existing healthcare focus) as a separate event in year one. Measure track cohesion. If the data supports it, restructure the full Connect summit into the parallel-track format the following year.

## Risks

**Risk: cross-sector relationship density (a real benefit of the current format) reduces under the track structure.** Mitigation: the shared plenary and the closing dinner preserve some cross-sector networking. Attendees who want more cross-sector interaction can attend cross-track sessions. The track structure does not eliminate cross-sector interaction. It just defaults to within-track time as the primary use.

**Risk: NEA's identity as a multi-sector firm is undermined by a summit structure that emphasizes vertical separation.** Mitigation: the firm's identity is reinforced by the shared plenary and by the managing partners' presence across tracks. The track structure is a programming choice, not an identity statement.

**Risk: smaller tracks (deep tech, fintech) have insufficient cohort size to function as standalone peer cohorts.** Mitigation: smaller tracks accept that they will have a smaller cohort experience and program accordingly. A track of `~40 attendees` works for peer cohorts of eight to ten at workshops. A track of `~20 attendees` does not work as a separate event and should be merged into an adjacent track for that year.

**Risk: managing partners and lead general partners spread thin across five tracks instead of being concentrated in one venue.** Mitigation: managing partners attend the shared plenary, then rotate through tracks during the rest of the summit. Track-lead partners stay in their track full-time. Coverage is distributed, not centralized, which is the point.

**Risk: cross-track sessions become poorly attended because attendees prefer to stay in their track.** Mitigation: limit cross-track sessions to one per summit, on a genuinely cross-cutting topic. Make attendance optional. The cross-track session is supplementary, not foundational.

## Open questions

How does the track structure interact with the firm's verticalization of partners? NEA has been building partner specialization by vertical for years. The summit's track structure aligns with this existing specialization rather than introducing a new dimension. Track-lead partners are the same partners who already lead the firm's sector teams.

What about portfolio companies whose vertical is genuinely new or doesn't fit existing tracks (climate, defense, education, frontier tech beyond deep tech)? Assign to the closest existing track for the year, with a noted observation. If the count of such companies grows to where they need their own track (say, ten or more portfolio companies in a not-fit category), add a sixth track the following year.

How does the format compose with the firm's other portfolio engagement (board meetings, partner office hours, the ongoing operating-partner support)? Compositionally well. The summit is the annual flagship convening. The rest is ongoing engagement. The track structure makes the summit a more useful annual event without changing the other engagement layers.

Should the firm's prospective investments attend the summit? Probably not at scale. The summit is a portfolio-only event. The few prospective founders who do attend (typically with explicit partner sponsorship) should attend a single track relevant to their company.

What about scaling beyond the current portfolio size? If NEA's portfolio grows further over the next five years, the tracks may need to subdivide (the healthcare track splits into biotech and digital health, for example). The structure supports this evolution because tracks are independent units that can be added or subdivided as the portfolio grows.
