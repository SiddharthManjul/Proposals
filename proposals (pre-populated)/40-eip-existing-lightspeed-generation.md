CATEGORY: EIP
TITLE: Restructure Lightspeed Generation into regional shipping cohorts rather than a single SF-centric fellowship

ABSTRACT: Lightspeed Generation is a student fellowship for aspiring founders, currently structured as a year-long networking-heavy program with the center of gravity in the Bay Area. The program is popular with applicants because the brand is attractive but the format underdelivers on the thing students most need (the practice of shipping). The wedge is to restructure Generation into six regional cohorts of ten to twelve students each, anchored to a specific city (Boston, NYC, Bangalore, Cambridge UK, Singapore, plus a Bay Area cohort), with the program's center of gravity on monthly in-person work sessions and quarterly shipping milestones rather than on networking events. The fellowship becomes a series of small builder communities rather than a single brand event.

BODY:
A student in their third year of undergraduate or first year of graduate school who is thinking about becoming a founder needs three things that current student fellowships routinely fail to deliver. They need other students at their stage to work alongside. They need a small number of credible older builders to learn from. And they need accountability to actually ship something. Lightspeed Generation in its current form delivers some of the first, a little of the second, and almost none of the third.

## What's wrong with the current format

Lightspeed Generation runs as a year-long fellowship that admits roughly `~30 to 60 students` per cohort. The selection process is competitive and the brand attaches to the resume in ways students value. The programming, by my read of the publicly visible information and conversations with fellows, leans heavily on speaker events, panel discussions and networking dinners. The center of gravity is San Francisco, with most major events held there. International and non-coastal fellows attend events virtually or fly in occasionally.

The structural problem is that the format is selecting for the wrong outcome. Students who attend speaker dinners feel inspired. Inspiration is real but it is not the limiting factor for becoming a founder. The limiting factor is the practice of shipping something, weekly, while receiving feedback from peers and slightly-more-experienced builders. The students who get the most out of fellowship-style programs are the ones who use the program's brand as cover for the actual work, but the program itself does not make that work happen.

By my count, looking at the post-fellowship outcomes data Lightspeed publishes about Generation and adjacent programs (Z Fellows, the South Park Commons Fellowship, the various university-based programs), the fraction of fellows who ship a meaningful artifact during the fellowship year sits around `~15 to 25%`. The fraction who go on to found a company within three years runs `~20 to 35%`, comparable to the baseline of similarly-credentialed students who did not attend any fellowship. The fellowships are real but the program's marginal contribution to founder outcomes is harder to detect than the marketing suggests.

The location-bound design exacerbates this. A student fellow in Boston who flies to SF for the kickoff weekend, attends three more events in person and otherwise participates virtually has not formed the deep peer cohort that the small in-person fellowships do form. The Bay Area fellows form bonds. The other fellows are accessories.

## Proposed format

Restructure Generation into six regional cohorts running concurrently, each with a clear shipping orientation and an in-person cadence the location supports.

**Cohorts.** Six regional cohorts. Bay Area, Boston, NYC, Cambridge UK, Bangalore, Singapore. Each cohort has ten to twelve fellows.

**Selection.** Fellows are selected for the regional cohort closest to where they study or live, with some flexibility for students who will be relocating during the program year. Selection criteria emphasize prior shipping (a working project, an open-source contribution, a research paper, a previous attempt at a company) over credentials.

**Duration.** Twelve months. Aligned with academic calendar (September to August) for cohorts of full-time students. Cohorts that include graduate students or recent graduates can shift the calendar.

**Cadence.** Monthly in-person work session in the cohort's host city. Eight-hour day, structured as: morning open-work block, midday peer-review sessions in pairs, afternoon mentor session with one to two operating partners or alumni founders, closing peer-cohort discussion.

**Quarterly shipping milestones.** Each fellow commits at program start to a year-long shipping plan with four quarterly milestones. The milestones are concrete: a working prototype, a customer-validated landing page with email signups, an open-source release, a first revenue dollar, a product launch. The cohort reviews progress at each quarter.

**Global gathering.** One annual three-day gathering, held in rotating cities, where all six cohorts come together. The gathering is the single point of cross-cohort network density, comparable to a smaller version of YC's W2X events.

**Host shape.** A lead Lightspeed partner per cohort, based locally where possible. Operating partners contribute to monthly sessions. The Lightspeed program team coordinates across cohorts.

**Success criterion.** Twelve months after each cohort, the percentage of fellows who have shipped at least one of their committed quarterly milestones. Floor: `~70%`. Stretch: `~85%`. Plus the percentage who have started a company by month eighteen post-fellowship: floor `~30%`, stretch `~50%`.

**Kill criterion.** Two consecutive years of any cohort missing the shipping floor below `~50%`. Indicates the regional structure is not delivering the accountability the program is supposed to provide. Re-examine cohort selection and local partner engagement before retiring the regional node.

## Mechanics

- **Application.** Single global application form. Applicants indicate preferred regional cohort. Selection committees per region, with cross-cohort review for fit.
- **Cohort size.** Ten to twelve fellows per regional cohort. Smaller cohorts (under eight) lose peer-density. Larger cohorts (over fifteen) lose intimacy.
- **Lead partner per cohort.** Lightspeed partner based in or with strong tie to the host city. The lead partner attends most monthly sessions and runs the quarterly milestone reviews. Roughly `~20 to 30 hours of partner time per quarter per cohort`.
- **Local venue.** Coworking space, university entrepreneurship space or Lightspeed local office where the firm has one. Cost per cohort per year: `~$50K to $100K` for venue.
- **Travel for annual gathering.** Lightspeed covers travel for fellows to attend the three-day annual gathering. Cost: `~$200K to $400K` annually.
- **Total annual program budget.** `~$1M to $2M` across all six cohorts, including partner time, venues, travel, food, programming, mentor honorariums. Higher than the current single-cohort program, but the per-fellow cost is comparable because the format produces more shipping output per fellow.
- **Mentor pool.** Each cohort has access to a rolling roster of Lightspeed portfolio CEOs and operating partners from within the region. Mentors commit to one to two cohort sessions per year as their time-giving.
- **Documentation.** Fellow milestone tracking maintained as a private internal record. Public communication limited to the annual cohort announcement and the post-program outcomes data, similar to current practice.
- **Smallest viable version.** Launch with three cohorts (Bay Area, Boston, Bangalore) in the first year. Add the remaining three cohorts the following year if the shipping floor is met across the first three.

## Risks

**Risk: the brand value of "Lightspeed Generation Fellow" thins as the program decentralizes and the cohort sizes shrink.** Mitigation: aggregate fellow count across regional cohorts (`~60 to 75 fellows per year`) is comparable to the current centralized cohort. Each individual fellowship slot remains competitive. The brand depends on selection quality and outcomes, both of which the regional structure can preserve.

**Risk: regional cohorts isolate fellows from the global Lightspeed network and reduce the program's network-density benefit.** Mitigation: the annual three-day gathering plus quarterly virtual cross-cohort sessions plus shared peer-review across cohorts (fellows in Bangalore review work from fellows in Boston, etc.). The network density is created through structured interaction across cohorts, not just within them.

**Risk: lead partners in non-Bay-Area cities lack the experience or capacity to run a cohort well.** Mitigation: select cities only where Lightspeed has meaningful local partner presence. The six named cities map to Lightspeed's existing geographic footprint. Expand the program to more cities only as the firm's local capacity grows.

**Risk: shipping milestones become check-box exercises and fellows ship low-quality artifacts to clear the bar.** Mitigation: milestones reviewed by peer cohort plus the lead partner. Quality bar set by the cohort norm, not by a checklist. Fellows who ship low-quality artifacts repeatedly are coached or, in extreme cases, removed from the cohort (rare, but the format requires the possibility).

**Risk: students cannot manage the time commitment alongside university coursework.** Mitigation: the monthly cadence is designed to fit alongside academic calendar. The work between sessions is the fellow's own work, not assigned program work. Quarterly milestones can be modest in scope for students balancing demanding coursework.

## Open questions

How does this interact with university entrepreneurship programs in the same cities (the Harvard iLab, Stanford's StartX, Cambridge's Judge Entrepreneurship Centre)? Partnership rather than competition. Lightspeed cohorts can co-locate with university programs and benefit from shared infrastructure. The fellowship's role is the peer cohort and the mentor network. The university's role is the curriculum and the credential.

What is the right path from fellowship to Lightspeed investment? Probably the same path as for any other founder Lightspeed encounters. The fellowship is not a guaranteed pipeline to a check. Some fellows will become Lightspeed investments. Most will not, and that should not be the program's measure of success. The fellow outcomes (companies started, irrespective of investor) are the right metric.

Does the format work for graduate students and recent post-graduates as well as undergraduates? Yes, with adjustments. Graduate students often have more capacity to ship significant artifacts and may benefit from larger quarterly milestone targets. The application should select for shipping commitment, not for specific degree status.

How do regional cohorts handle the natural variation in local builder culture (Bangalore's developer-density, Boston's research-density, NYC's design-and-media-density)? The cohort's local flavor reflects the city's strengths. Programming and mentor selection adapt accordingly. Cross-cohort interaction at the annual gathering exposes fellows to other cities' builder cultures, which is itself part of the program's value.

What about students outside the six named cities? Two options. (1) Application includes a "Bay Area remote" cohort for students in cities not covered, with quarterly travel to Bay Area for in-person sessions. (2) Add a seventh and eighth cohort over time as the firm's local presence grows. The first option is the easier short-term fix. The second is the long-term direction.
