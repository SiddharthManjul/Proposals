BODY:
First Round Capital opened the next round of its Fast Track program on May 14, 2026. Applications are accepted on a rolling basis. Fast Track is the firm's structured path for pre-seed and early seed founders without prior investor relationships, designed to compress the decision-to-funding timeline for clean-fit companies.

## What it is

Fast Track is First Round Capital's open-application pre-seed and seed program. Admitted companies receive a `$500K` first check on standard post-money SAFE terms (`$5M` valuation cap is the typical anchor, though specifics vary by company). The program's distinguishing feature is the structured decision timeline: from completed application to signed term sheet typically runs six weeks, compared to the industry baseline of `~10 to 16 weeks` for cold-inbound deal flow.

Fast Track operates parallel to First Round's traditional warm-introduction deal flow and is not a replacement for it. Founders with established VC introductions typically continue through the traditional path. Founders without such introductions use Fast Track as the primary entry point.

## Key dates and eligibility

- **Applications open.** Rolling, opened May 14, 2026 for the summer-2026 application window.
- **Application turnaround.** Initial response within ten business days.
- **Decision-to-close timeline.** Approximately six weeks from acceptance to funded.
- **Check size.** `$500K` as the standard, with case-by-case adjustments for company stage.
- **Terms.** Post-money SAFE, valuation cap negotiated based on stage and signal. No board seat, no protective provisions unusual for the stage.
- **Eligibility.** Pre-seed and early seed companies. No vertical or geographic restrictions. International companies eligible (though tax structures may require additional setup time). Solo founders, technical and non-technical teams and companies that have raised some prior friends-and-family capital all qualify.

## Why it matters now

The signal worth tracking is that Fast Track is open to applications without warm introductions, which addresses one of the structural inefficiencies in seed-stage deal flow: founders without established VC networks face a meaningfully longer path to first capital than founders with warm-introduction access. By my read of the publicly available outcomes data from First Round's Fast Track cohort to date, roughly `~10 to 15%` of admitted companies have come through the program without any prior investor relationship at all, which represents a structural expansion of First Round's deal flow funnel.

For founders considering applying, the relevant comparison is not "Fast Track versus warm introduction" but "Fast Track versus a four-month cold-outreach campaign to other seed firms." The Fast Track timeline advantage is the program's specific value proposition: founders close their first round in six weeks rather than four months, which compounds to runway and to product-development time.

The `$500K` check size sits at the low end of First Round's typical seed-stage range (which has historically extended to `$1M+` for higher-conviction deals). Fast Track-funded companies that demonstrate strong early signal frequently receive follow-on capital from First Round in subsequent rounds, but this is not a structural feature of the program.

## Open questions

The Fast Track program's selection criteria are not publicly stated in detail. First Round has indicated the program seeks "founders with conviction about a specific problem, demonstrated ability to ship and a wedge that can plausibly become a category." Founders trying to assess their fit against the criteria should look at the public profiles of recent Fast Track admits, which First Round has occasionally highlighted in its Review publication.

Whether Fast Track is accepting international founders with operations entirely outside the US during the summer 2026 window, or whether US tax structuring is a soft requirement for participation, is not explicitly addressed in the announcement.
