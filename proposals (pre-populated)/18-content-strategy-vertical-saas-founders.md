CATEGORY: CIP
TITLE: Take the trade publication's column instead of building a vertical-SaaS blog

ABSTRACT: Vertical SaaS founders (dental practice management, restaurant POS, contractor CRM, multifamily property tools) try to build their own audience the way generic SaaS does: company blog, LinkedIn posts, paid ads. The audience they need does not follow startup-style content. Operators in the vertical read three things: their trade publication, their vertical community (Facebook group, Slack, association forum) and the consultants they hire. The wedge is to be the credible written voice inside the trade publication, not to compete against it.

BODY:
The mid-market dental practice owner does not read Hacker News. They read *Dental Economics*. The independent restaurant operator does not subscribe to a16z Andreessen-Horowitz substacks. They read *Restaurant Business* and *QSR Magazine*. The general contractor running a $4M-revenue construction business does not click on LinkedIn thought-leader posts. They read *Builder Online* and the state-level builder-association newsletter. Every vertical has its publication. The publication has the audience. Founders in the vertical keep trying to build a parallel audience from scratch and keep losing.

## What's wrong now

The typical vertical-SaaS company at Series A or B has built a content team of two or three: a content manager, a writer, sometimes a part-time SEO specialist. They publish to the company blog two or three times a week. The articles target generic search queries (*"how to run payroll for a dental practice"*, *"restaurant menu engineering tips"*). They write to rank, not to inform.

By my reading of public traffic data and the founders I have talked to, the typical vertical-SaaS company blog reaches `~3,000 to 12,000` monthly unique visitors after eighteen months of consistent publishing. Conversion to demo request is `0.4 to 0.9%`. Cost per demo request through content sits around `$120 to $280`, after the content team's fully-loaded cost.

The trade publication for the same vertical has `50,000 to 250,000` monthly readers, in exactly the buyer segment, with reader trust built over decades. Its content slots open up regularly because trade publications are starved for credible written contributors who are not the publication's own staff.

The math is one-sided. The vertical SaaS company spends `$240K/year` on a content team that reaches `100K reader-impressions/year` in the buyer audience. The trade-publication column, written by the founder at maybe `$4K/month` in author time, reaches `$5M+` reader-impressions/year and arrives with the publication's editorial credibility attached.

## The proposed change

Stop publishing to the company blog as the primary channel. Negotiate a monthly column at the dominant trade publication for the vertical. The founder is the bylined author. The column is operational content, not product pitches.

What changes versus today: the channel (the trade publication, not your blog), the audience (the publication's full readership, not your SEO surface), the voice (operator-to-operator, not marketing-to-prospect) and the conversion path (column reader contacts you directly because you sound credible, not because they clicked an ad).

The column's topic mix should be roughly: 40% operational best practices observed across the company's customer base (anonymized), 25% regulatory or industry-trend interpretation, 20% case studies of one customer's specific operational improvement, 15% commentary on commonly-asked questions in the vertical's community. Zero percent product pitches. The byline credit is the marketing.

## Mechanics

- **Identify the publication.** For each vertical there are usually 2 to 4 candidate publications. Sort by readership in your specific buyer segment, by editorial credibility (trade-press critics are blunt) and by acceptance of outside contributors. Pick one. Some examples: dental: *Dental Economics*, *DentistryIQ*. Restaurants: *QSR Magazine*, *FSR Magazine*. Construction: *Builder Online*, *Engineering News-Record*. Property management: *Multi-Housing News*, *Units Magazine*.
- **Approach.** Email the publication's editor with three sample column ideas and one finished sample piece (`~1,200 words`). Reference your operational visibility (*"we serve 800 dental practices across 30 states"*). Most trade-pub editors will respond inside two weeks. Acceptance rate when the pitch is good is `60 to 80%`. The publication does not usually pay much (`$0 to $400/column`), but the byline credit is the deal.
- **Author.** Founder for the first six months. Once cadence is established, share the byline with a domain-credible employee (a former practitioner you have hired into customer success or product, not a hired writer).
- **Cadence.** One column per month, `1,200 to 1,800 words`.
- **Budget.** Founder time at `8 to 12 hours/month` of writing and editing. Optionally a part-time editor at `$1,500 to $3,000/month` to polish. Total: `$20 to $40K/year`. Versus the content team's `$240K/year`, this is a `90% cost reduction` at higher reach.
- **Repurpose the column.** Each piece becomes: the trade-publication article (canonical), a monthly email to your customer and prospect list, two LinkedIn posts that quote the strongest passages, one short video where the founder summarizes the key argument for the publication's social channels. The same content does five jobs.
- **Newsletter.** The same audience signs up for your customer/prospect newsletter through the column's byline. Monthly send. The newsletter retains buyers who would otherwise read the column once and forget you wrote it.
- **Smallest viable version.** Land one guest piece. Track inbound. If a single column piece generates `>5` inbound conversations from buyer-segment readers, commit to the monthly column. If it generates zero, the publication or the topic was wrong.
- **Traction metric.** Qualified inbound conversations per published column. Floor: 5 per piece. Stretch: 20 per piece.
- **Retention metric.** Customer NPS lift attributable to the founder's public visibility in the vertical. Harder to measure, but customers cite the trade-pub presence as a credibility signal in churn surveys.

## Open questions

Trade publications are increasingly behind paywalls. *Dental Economics* is freely readable. *Engineering News-Record* charges `$400/year`. Does the column reach the right audience if the publication is paywalled? Partial answer: yes, because paywalled publications often have the highest-intent reader base. But the social-share reach drops sharply. For paywalled venues, the in-publication column is the credibility signal and the founder's own newsletter is the volume play.

What is the analogous strategy for verticals without a strong trade publication? Some verticals (early-stage categories, very fragmented industries) lack a clear publication leader. In that case, the analog is the dominant podcast or the dominant association's newsletter. The mechanics are similar: be the credible contributor, not the competitor.

When does the column-as-marketing strategy stop working? Roughly when the founder no longer has time to write it personally and the bylined-author substitute is visibly less credible. That usually coincides with Series C, by which point the company should have other distribution channels working.

How does this strategy compose with content the company runs on its own properties? Probably the company blog should retain only product-update and customer-success content (low-volume, high-relevance to existing customers). The marketing content lives at the trade publication. Don't try to do both in parallel.
