CATEGORY: EIP
TITLE: Split Insight Onsite into stage-specific summits so seed and Series A founders get peer cohorts

ABSTRACT: Insight Onsite is the operating-partner-led portfolio summit organized by Insight Partners. The format has been refined over many years and the operating partner programming is genuinely strong. The structural problem is that Insight's portfolio now spans seed-stage companies through public-company-scale operators, and the single-summit format groups all of them together. The wedge is to split Onsite into three stage-specific summits running on staggered cadences: Onsite Early (seed and Series A), Onsite Growth (Series B and Series C), Onsite Scale (Series D and beyond). Each summit has its own peer cohort, its own programming intensity and its own operating-partner pairings. The Onsite brand stays. The cohort matching gets right.

BODY:
Insight Partners' Onsite program is unusual among VC operating teams in two respects. The Onsite organization is large (substantial operating-partner headcount across functions). The programming is genuinely operational rather than sales-and-relationship-flavored. Both make Onsite an effective program. But the program's main convening event currently groups together founders at radically different stages, which dilutes the peer-learning dimension of what is otherwise a strong format.

## What's wrong with the current format

Insight Onsite's annual summit currently runs as a multi-day gathering for the firm's broad portfolio. Attendees include founders and executives from across Insight's portfolio, with stage ranging from seed companies the firm invested in early to public-company-scale operators where Insight may be a meaningful shareholder. By my count, the typical summit has `~400 to 700 attendees` across stages, with programming structured around shared sessions plus optional breakouts.

The problem is that the operational challenges of a `$5M ARR` company and the operational challenges of a `$500M ARR` company are different by category, not just by scale. The seed-stage founder is wrestling with product-market fit, the first ten sales hires and whether to ship the next major release before or after the Series A close. The Series D operator is wrestling with international expansion, M&A integration and the public-company-readiness audit. Sessions that try to serve both audiences either pitch to the average attendee (and serve no one well) or split into stage-specific tracks (in which case the cohort is already de facto stage-split, just inside a venue that pretends it is unified).

By my read of attendee feedback patterns at multi-stage summits across the VC industry, the peer-learning value drops sharply when the stage range across attendees exceeds roughly two adjacent stages. Seed plus Series A works as a cohort. Series A plus Series B plus Series C does not, because the operational problems diverge too far. Insight Onsite's current format covers four to five stages in one room.

The cost of this structure shows up indirectly. Seed-stage founders attend Onsite and form weak peer relationships because the dominant attendee cohort is at a different stage. Public-company-scale operators attend and find themselves in sessions targeted at Series C, missing the peer-cohort experience they would value most. The middle-stage attendees get the best value but the format's design dilutes the experience for the populations at either end.

## Proposed format

Split Onsite into three stage-specific summits, each running annually on a staggered calendar.

**Onsite Early.** Seed-stage and Series A founders. Roughly `~120 to 200 attendees`. Two-day summit, scheduled in spring.

**Onsite Growth.** Series B and Series C founders and CEOs. Roughly `~150 to 250 attendees`. Three-day summit, scheduled in late summer.

**Onsite Scale.** Series D and beyond, including pre-IPO and public-company-scale operators. Roughly `~80 to 150 attendees`. Two-day summit, scheduled in late fall.

**Programming intensity differs per summit.** Early has the highest programming density (peer-cohort focused, structured workshops on product-market-fit and the seed-to-Series-A transition). Growth has the most operator-partner involvement (the firm's deepest expertise lives in scaling from Series B to Series C, so this is where the operating partners spend most of their summit-related time). Scale has the lightest programming and the most relationship-density-focused format (the cohort is small and the value comes from peer time, not from sessions).

**Cross-summit thread.** One shared event per year, ideally a half-day, where attendees from all three summits gather virtually or in person for one specific cross-stage topic (e.g., "lessons from this year's IPO cohort", "the AI transformation across stages"). This preserves the brand cohesion of Onsite as a single program with three tracks rather than three disconnected events.

**Programming format per summit.** Each summit uses the same structural shape: ninety-minute peer-cohort workshops as the core, operator-partner-led deep-dives on specific functional topics, free time for informal peer conversation, off-the-record rules throughout.

**Host shape.** Onsite's existing operating-partner team distributes across the three summits. Functional operating partners (Sales, Marketing, Engineering, Customer Success, Finance) attend whichever summit their function is most relevant to at each stage.

**Success criterion per summit.** Cohort attendance rate (do attendees come back the following year) and peer-relationship persistence at six months post-summit. Floor: `~70% repeat-attendance`. Stretch: `~85%`. Peer-relationship persistence floor: `>50% of attendees report at least two ongoing relationships from the summit`.

**Kill criterion.** Two consecutive summits at any stage with NPS below `45` or peer-relationship persistence below `~35%`. Re-examine the stage definitions and the programming density before retiring the format at that stage.

## Mechanics

- **Stage assignment.** Each portfolio company assigned to a stage based on revenue, funding round and operational complexity. Stage assignment reviewed annually. Some companies straddle stages and attend the closer one for their year.
- **Cross-stage invitations.** Some attendees benefit from attending a stage other than their own (a Series A CEO preparing for the Series B transition might benefit from Onsite Growth). The program allows a small number of cross-stage invitations per summit, typically `~5 to 10` per event.
- **Calendar.** Three summits per year, spaced roughly four months apart. Spring Early, late-summer Growth, late-fall Scale. Calendar designed to avoid conflict with major industry events and quarterly earnings cycles for the larger portfolio companies.
- **Venue.** Each summit at its own venue, sized appropriately. Early at a mid-size venue (`~120 to 200 attendee capacity`). Growth at a larger destination property. Scale at a small intimate venue.
- **Budget.** Three summits cost more in aggregate than one (`~50 to 80%` more), roughly `~$3M to $5M annually` versus the current `~$2M to $3M`. The increase reflects two additional venue rentals and additional operating-partner time. The per-attendee cost stays similar.
- **Operating-partner time.** Total Onsite operating-partner time spent on summits roughly doubles, from `~6 to 8 weeks per year` to `~12 to 16 weeks per year` across the team. Justified by the increased portfolio engagement depth.
- **Smallest viable version.** Split off Onsite Early as a separate summit in year one, while leaving Growth and Scale combined as the current format. Measure peer-cohort value gains at Onsite Early. If meaningful, split Onsite Scale off the following year, leaving Growth as the standalone third summit.

## Risks

**Risk: portfolio companies whose stage is ambiguous (a fast-growing Series A that is operationally already at Series B scale, for example) fit awkwardly.** Mitigation: stage assignment reviewed with the portfolio CEO themselves. Cross-stage invitations available for genuine straddlers. Flexibility on assignment with clear default rules.

**Risk: the cross-summit thread feels like a forced unification rather than a genuine cohort event.** Mitigation: the thread should be optional and lightly structured. One half-day or evening event per year. Not a third or fourth distinct summit forcing attendees to add another travel commitment.

**Risk: the brand of Onsite dilutes across three sub-summits and the overall recognition of the program decreases.** Mitigation: all three summits retain the Onsite brand. Marketing positions them as three tracks of the same program, not as different programs. The cross-summit thread reinforces the brand cohesion.

**Risk: operating partners become overstretched across three summits per year and the program quality drops.** Mitigation: programming intensity matched to stage (Early and Scale have lighter programming than Growth). Operating-partner attendance staggered across summits.

**Risk: the firm's portfolio at certain stages is too small to support a peer-cohort summit (especially Onsite Scale, where the portfolio at Series D and beyond may be `~30 to 80 companies` rather than the hundreds at earlier stages).** Mitigation: cohort sizes match portfolio sizes. Onsite Scale at `~80 to 150 attendees` works for the firm's late-stage portfolio. If the late-stage cohort grows too small to support a summit, merge Scale into Growth as the bottom-half of an expanded summit.

## Open questions

How does the split format affect Insight's deal-sourcing for new investments? Mostly preserved. Onsite is a portfolio event, not a deal-sourcing event in its primary purpose. The few prospective-founder attendees who do attend (typically alumni or referrals) attend the stage most relevant to their company. The format change does not change this.

Should there be a fourth tier for the firm's public-company portfolio (where Insight may still hold meaningful positions)? Probably not. Public-company executives operate in a different time-budget regime and a multi-day off-site summit competes poorly with their other obligations. A shorter, focused single-day event in NYC or SF for the public-company portfolio is the right format, separate from Onsite proper.

What about the operating-partner content that currently runs across the full summit (the playbooks, the operational frameworks)? These continue, distributed across the three summits with stage-appropriate adaptations. The "first ten sales hires" playbook is for Onsite Early. The "scaling sales operations to $200M ARR" playbook is for Onsite Growth. The "preparing for public-company-grade financial reporting" playbook is for Onsite Scale. Same underlying content shape, stage-specific adaptations.

How does this format interact with industry-wide stage-specific events that already exist (SaaStr Annual for SaaS founders at scale, the various AI-specific summits)? Onsite stays portfolio-only. The industry events are open. Some Onsite attendees will attend both. The portfolio-only setting of Onsite is the structural advantage that justifies the program's existence even with industry events available.

Should Insight publish its summit content externally (operating playbooks, recorded sessions)? Probably continue current practice of selective external publication. Some operational content has marketing value if published. Most works better as portfolio-exclusive material that reinforces the value of being an Insight portfolio company.
