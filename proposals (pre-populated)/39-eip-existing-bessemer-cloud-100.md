CATEGORY: EIP
TITLE: Add a pre-summit day at the Bessemer Cloud 100 for seed and Series A SaaS founders

ABSTRACT: The Bessemer Cloud 100 Summit and its associated Forbes list are the canonical celebration of late-stage private cloud companies. The list is well-curated, the summit is well-run and the audience that already runs `$100M+ ARR` companies gets meaningful peer learning from each other. The seed and Series A SaaS founders who would benefit most from spending a day learning from those companies are not in the room because the summit is invitation-only and stage-gated. The wedge is to add a pre-summit day, the day before the main event, structured as a seed-to-Series-A SaaS workshop where Cloud 100 alumni serve as mentors and operators. The Cloud 100 brand stays exclusive. The early-stage learning channel opens.

BODY:
The Cloud 100 list is the most-respected ranking of private cloud companies because Bessemer applies real selection discipline and partners with Forbes for credibility. The summit that follows the list publication is the formal celebration: the listed founders gather, share notes and reinforce the relationships that make the cloud category function as a network. The summit is excellent and should not change. The opportunity is what happens around it.

## What's wrong with the current format

The Cloud 100 Summit currently runs as an invite-only event at a destination property, typically over two days. Attendees are the founders and CEOs of the Cloud 100 listed companies (and the previous year's listed companies, alumni) plus selected Bessemer partners and a small number of public-cloud-company executives. By my count, the attendee list runs roughly `~150 to 250 people`, virtually all of whom are leading companies in the `$50M ARR and above` range. The peer-learning between attendees is real because they are at similar stages and have similar problems.

The seed and Series A SaaS founders building toward becoming a Cloud 100 company in five to seven years are not at the summit. They learn about the summit through Forbes coverage and through portfolio communications from their own investors. The content of the summit is largely opaque to them. The relationships formed at the summit do not include them. The mentorship loop that could exist (a founder five years ahead of you, willing to spend an hour explaining how they navigated the specific transition you are about to enter) does not have a structural surface.

This is not Bessemer's failure. The summit's exclusivity is part of what makes the peer cohort work. But the structural gap (no formal connection between the Cloud 100 cohort and the next-generation SaaS founders) is large. The cost of the gap shows up indirectly in the cloud category as a whole, where each generation of founders re-discovers the lessons the previous generation already learned, because the transmission channel is informal and ad-hoc.

By my read of the SaaS ecosystem's organizational learning, the most important transmission of operating knowledge happens through one-on-one founder-to-founder conversations, not through conferences or content. The Cloud 100 has the surface to facilitate that transmission at scale and does not currently use it.

## Proposed format

Add a pre-summit day, held the day before the main Cloud 100 Summit, at the same venue, structured as a seed-to-Series-A SaaS workshop where Cloud 100 alumni serve as mentors.

**Duration.** One day. Held the day before the main summit. Eight hours of programming.

**Cadence.** Annual, mirroring the main summit cadence. One pre-summit day per Cloud 100 cycle.

**Composition.** `~150 seed and Series A SaaS founders` plus `~30 to 50 Cloud 100 alumni mentors`. The mentors are drawn from the previous five years of Cloud 100 listed founders who have volunteered to participate.

**Founder selection.** Application-based. Open to seed and Series A SaaS founders globally. Selection criteria: existing company at the relevant stage (`$0 to $5M ARR`), demonstrated product-market signal and a specific question or topic the founder is currently navigating. Roughly `~2,000 to 4,000 applications expected`. Admit `~150`.

**Mentor selection.** Past Cloud 100 founders invited by Bessemer. Roughly thirty to fifty agree to participate annually. Mentors attend the main summit afterward as their primary reason for being there. The pre-summit day is the additional ask.

**Programming structure.** Morning: three concurrent workshops, ninety minutes each, on operational topics chosen by mentor feedback (e.g., "first ten enterprise customers", "the seed-to-Series-A handoff", "hiring the first VP of Engineering"). Each workshop seats fifty founders with eight to ten mentors as panelists and small-group leaders. Afternoon: structured one-on-one mentor-founder sessions, twenty minutes each, scheduled in advance based on topic interest. Evening: shared dinner with the main-summit attendees as they arrive for the next day's start.

**Off-the-record.** Chatham House rules throughout. No recording, no journalists, no live-streaming. The trust premise that makes mentor honesty possible is the same one that makes the main summit work.

**Host shape.** Bessemer partners run the programming. Mentor-founders are the content. Bessemer's role is convener, not lecturer.

**Success criterion.** Six months after the pre-summit day, founder-attendees report on the value they got (in a structured survey). Healthy: `>75%` report at least one ongoing mentor relationship from the day. `>50%` report at least one specific operational change they made because of a pre-summit conversation.

**Kill criterion.** Two consecutive cycles with NPS below `50` among founder-attendees, or mentor participation falling below `~20`. Indicates the format is not delivering value to either side.

## Mechanics

- **Application.** Open application on the Cloud 100 website. Opens four months before the summit. Reviewed by a small selection committee from Bessemer's SaaS team.
- **Mentor recruitment.** Bessemer's portfolio team reaches out to each year's incoming Cloud 100 cohort plus the previous four years' lists. Volunteer mentors confirm participation roughly two months before the event. Mentor-attendee matching done one week before the event based on workshop interest.
- **Venue.** Same venue as the main summit, day before. Marginal venue cost roughly `~$50K to $100K` (food, room rental, materials).
- **Founder cost.** `~$500 to $1,000` per founder for the day, covering food and program. Travel and accommodation on the founder. Bessemer can subsidize for founders with demonstrated cost constraints. The program should not exclude founders for affordability reasons.
- **Mentor compensation.** No payment. Mentors are doing this as their network-giving for the year. Bessemer covers their travel and accommodation as part of the main summit anyway, so the pre-summit day adds no direct expense for mentors.
- **Total budget per pre-summit day.** `~$150K to $300K` all-in. The founder ticket revenue at `~$500 to $1,000` per founder for `~150 founders` partially offsets. Net cost to Bessemer: `~$100K to $200K` per cycle, modest against the Cloud 100 program total.
- **Documentation.** No external documentation beyond Bessemer's own follow-up communications with attendees. The pre-summit day is not a marketing event.
- **Smallest viable version.** Run a pilot pre-summit day with `~60 founders` and `~15 mentors`. Measure satisfaction and follow-on relationships. Scale to the full size if the pilot's metrics support it.

## Risks

**Risk: the pre-summit day becomes a marketing channel for Bessemer's seed-stage investments and the mentor-founders feel used.** Mitigation: Bessemer's seed-stage deal-sourcing happens elsewhere. The pre-summit day is not pitched as a deal-flow event internally. Partners attending are explicitly told it is not the pipeline event of the week. Founders who attend are not preferentially evaluated for Bessemer seed-stage investment relative to other applicants in the firm's normal pipeline.

**Risk: the seed and Series A founders feel imposter-syndrome-y in proximity to Cloud 100 alumni.** Mitigation: programming explicitly structured to put founders in conversation with mentors as peers-in-different-stages, not as supplicants. Mentors trained briefly (`~30 minutes` of preparation) on conversation norms.

**Risk: Cloud 100 mentor-attendees treat the pre-summit day as a tax they pay before the real summit and disengage.** Mitigation: mentor selection biased toward founders who have explicitly volunteered to help (not pressured into it). Mentor experience surveyed after the event. Mentors who report low satisfaction are not re-invited. The program quality depends on mentor engagement, not on mentor coverage.

**Risk: the founder selection process becomes politicized and the "right" founders (network-connected, well-known) crowd out the founders who would actually benefit most.** Mitigation: explicit selection criteria. Geographic and demographic diversity. Application review by a committee that includes external advisors (former Cloud 100 founders themselves), not solely Bessemer partners.

**Risk: the pre-summit day reduces the exclusivity feel of the main Cloud 100 Summit.** Mitigation: the two events are clearly separated (different attendee badges, different programming, different rooms). The main summit's exclusivity is preserved. The pre-summit day is positioned as adjacent rather than as opening act.

## Open questions

Should the pre-summit day's mentor pool include current Cloud 100 attendees as well as alumni? Probably alumni-first, current-list-optional. Current Cloud 100 attendees are at the summit for their own reasons and adding the mentor ask might overload the schedule. Some current-list founders will volunteer naturally. Accept them when they do.

How does this format compose with Bessemer's existing seed-stage programming (the BVP Forge accelerator-adjacent program, the seed-stage portfolio support, the State of the Cloud annual report)? The pre-summit day is the gathering event. The existing programs are the ongoing engagement layers. The pre-summit day is also a natural recruiting surface for the deeper programs.

What about non-SaaS adjacent founders (vertical AI, infrastructure, hardware-with-software)? The Cloud 100 list itself is broadening to include AI-native companies, so the founder selection for the pre-summit day should follow. The right framing for the day is "cloud-and-AI seed and Series A SaaS" rather than pure-SaaS.

How does the format work in a non-summit year if Bessemer ever pauses or restructures the main Cloud 100 Summit? The pre-summit day depends on the main summit's existence. If the main summit pauses, the pre-summit day pauses with it. The format does not stand alone.

What is the right relationship between the pre-summit day and Forbes (the media partner on the main Cloud 100 list)? Probably minimal. The pre-summit day is a Bessemer-run founder event, not a Forbes-co-branded one. Forbes coverage of the main summit and the list continues. The pre-summit day is intentionally off-the-record and out of press coverage.
