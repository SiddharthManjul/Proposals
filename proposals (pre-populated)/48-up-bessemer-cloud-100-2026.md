BODY:
Bessemer Venture Partners confirmed the publication date and nomination window for the 2026 edition of the Cloud 100 list on May 7, 2026. The list publishes July 28, 2026, with nominations open through June 30. The list is published jointly with Forbes and is the canonical annual ranking of private cloud companies.

## What it is

The Cloud 100 is an annual ranking of the top 100 private cloud companies, scored on a combined methodology that weighs market valuation, revenue growth, customer signal and operational metrics. The list is co-produced by Bessemer Venture Partners and Forbes, with the editorial and methodology lead held by Bessemer. The list publication is accompanied by an invitation-only summit held later in the summer for the listed founders, their alumni and a small cohort of Bessemer partners.

## Key dates and eligibility

- **Nominations open.** May 7, 2026.
- **Nominations close.** June 30, 2026.
- **List publication.** July 28, 2026 (in partnership with Forbes).
- **Cloud 100 Summit.** Late August 2026, date to be confirmed approximately three weeks ahead. Held at a destination property, invitation-only.
- **Eligibility.** Private cloud companies. Public companies are not eligible (companies that have IPO'd during the eligibility window are reviewed case by case). Annual recurring revenue typically above `$50M` for inclusion in serious contention, though the exact methodology weight varies year to year. International companies eligible. Companies based primarily on infrastructure, application software, vertical SaaS or AI-native cloud delivery are all in scope.

## Why it matters now

The Cloud 100 is a publication credential that materially affects company valuation conversations, talent recruitment and follow-on capital availability. Listed companies typically experience measurable lifts in inbound interest from late-stage funds, late-stage talent and acquisition-curious public companies in the months following publication. By my read of historical patterns, roughly `~80 to 90%` of Cloud 100 listed companies in the prior five years have either completed Series E or later financings or executed exits within four years of their first listing.

For 2026 specifically, the signal worth tracking is whether the list's composition shifts toward AI-native cloud companies. The 2024 and 2025 lists already included a meaningful share of AI infrastructure plays. The 2026 list is widely expected to include more AI-native application companies as well as the infrastructure layer. The methodology has historically been weighted toward demonstrated revenue rather than promised revenue, which suggests AI companies with strong revenue traction (not just product demonstrations) are favored.

Founders building toward Cloud 100 inclusion should treat the nomination window as a reminder of where their company stands relative to the list's roughly `$50M+ ARR` informal floor. Nomination does not require Bessemer relationship. The form is open and self-nomination is accepted, though most listed companies are surfaced through investor or analyst nomination.

## Open questions

The exact methodology weights between revenue growth, total revenue, valuation, customer satisfaction and operational metrics are not publicly disclosed in detail. Bessemer has historically published the high-level dimensions but not the specific scoring. Whether the 2026 methodology gives differential weight to AI-native companies (or to specific revenue-quality metrics that distinguish AI revenue from traditional SaaS) is unstated.

The Cloud 100 Summit attendee list has historically been roughly `~150 to 250 attendees`, mostly listed founders, alumni from prior lists and Bessemer partners. Whether the 2026 summit expands attendee scope (to include other VC partners, public-company executives or analyst guests) is unclarified in the announcement.
