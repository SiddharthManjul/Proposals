CATEGORY: EIP
TITLE: Add a paid cohort track to YC Startup School with eight-person peer groups and twelve-week commitments

ABSTRACT: YC Startup School in its current shape is a content library with a thin cohort overlay. Anyone can join, the curriculum is mostly recorded lectures and the small-group matching that does exist asks little of participants in return. Completion rates are low. The peer relationships founders form are mostly forgotten by month three. The wedge is to keep the existing open program free and add a paid (or accountability-gated) Cohort Track of eight-person peer groups with weekly assignments, twelve-week duration, vertical-specific matching and a real graduation. The Open Track stays as a content funnel. The Cohort Track becomes the actual learning surface.

BODY:
YC Startup School promises "free online courses on starting a startup." It delivers exactly that: video lectures, a community forum, and a periodic email digest. What it does not deliver is the thing the lectures themselves describe as the core founder unlock, which is a small group of peers who hold you accountable for the work. The asymmetry is visible in the completion data and in the fact that most founders who finish Startup School describe it the way one would describe a Coursera class.

## What's wrong with the current format

Walk through the experience of a typical Startup School enrollee. They sign up because someone in their network recommended it or because they searched for "how to start a startup." They get a curriculum of `~12 recorded lectures` plus access to a community of `~50,000+ participants`. They are assigned to a "Founder Group" of roughly eight to ten people, matched loosely by stage and interest. They have a Slack channel.

By my read, in any given Startup School cohort, completion rates of the lecture series run somewhere between `~12 and 22%`. Active participation in Founder Groups after week three drops to `~10 to 15%` of original members. The conversion from Startup School participant to a YC core-program applicant runs at around `~5 to 8%`. Those conversion numbers are roughly fine for a free funnel program, but they understate the loss: the founders who enrolled with real intent to ship a company and used Startup School as their primary learning resource are mostly underserved by the format.

The structural problem is that nothing in the program asks the founder for commitment. The lectures are recorded so there is no real-time obligation. The Founder Groups have no facilitator and no assignments. The community Slack channel is too large to function as a peer cohort. The result is a program that gates nothing, asks little and gets correspondingly little engagement.

This is fine for the brand-building purpose (YC reaches `50,000+ founders per year` who would otherwise never have a relationship with it) but the founders who would have been most served by a real cohort experience are exactly the ones the loose format underserves most.

## Proposed format

Keep YC Startup School's Open Track exactly as it is today (free, content-driven, the funnel layer). Add a new Cohort Track alongside it with the following structure.

**Cohort size.** Eight founders per cohort. Smaller is fragile against absences. Larger erodes the trust required for honest conversation. Eight is the established peer-group size from analogous formats (Vistage forums, Reboot's group format, the EO Forum).

**Duration.** Twelve weeks per cycle. Three cycles per year (winter, spring, fall). The duration matches the YC core-program duration intentionally, so Cohort Track graduates have a natural transition into the core-program application pipeline if they choose.

**Cadence.** Weekly cohort meeting, two hours, video call. Plus weekly assignment (a public artifact: a customer-conversation summary, a feature shipped, a metric logged, a build-in-public update). Plus one office-hour drop-in per month from a YC partner.

**Composition.** Cohorts matched by vertical (devtools, consumer, B2B SaaS, marketplace, healthtech, fintech, deep tech) and by stage (pre-product, post-product-pre-revenue, post-revenue-pre-scale). Vertical matching is the more important variable.

**Facilitator.** Each cohort has a facilitator who is a recent YC alumnus (last two to four batches), paid `~$3,000 to $5,000` for the twelve-week commitment. Not a YC partner. The facilitator's job is to keep the cohort meetings on agenda, surface contributions from quieter members and enforce the no-pitch norm.

**Accountability gate.** Cohort Track requires either a paid commitment (`~$500 to $1,500` per founder for the twelve weeks) or a verified accountability commitment (founder publicly commits to attendance and to shipping the weekly artifact). The paid option is the simpler default. The accountability-gated option exists for founders who cannot pay but can credibly commit.

**Success criterion.** Cohort completion rate (founder attends ten or more of the twelve weekly meetings and submits eight or more of the twelve weekly artifacts). Floor: `70%`. Stretch: `85%`. Plus follow-on conversion to YC core-program application within twelve months: floor `~35%`, stretch `~55%`.

**Kill criterion.** Two consecutive cycles with completion below `50%` indicates the cohort design is not delivering what it promises. Pause and re-examine.

## Mechanics

- **Application.** Open application form, reviewed by a small Startup School operations team. Selection criteria: stated intent to ship, existing or near-term company, willingness to commit to the twelve-week format. Roughly `~4,000 to 8,000 applications per cycle` expected if Cohort Track grows to scale. Selection takes the top `~600 to 1,200` per cycle (sizing for 75 to 150 cohorts).
- **Cohort matching.** Algorithmic on stage and vertical, with manual review for fit. Cohorts published two weeks before cycle start.
- **Facilitator recruitment.** Pulled from recent YC alumni who volunteer or who are nominated by their batch. Paid as contractors. One facilitator per cohort.
- **Partner office hours.** YC partners commit `~3 to 5 hours per partner per cycle` to drop into cohorts. Roughly one cohort per partner per cycle. Partners not assigned to specific cohorts but available across the cycle.
- **Budget.** Facilitator pay (`~$300K to $1M per cycle` depending on scale). Operations team time. Software (existing). Total annual incremental cost: `~$1.5M to $4M`. Revenue from paid track at scale: `~$1M to $3M`. Net cost: roughly cost-neutral to mildly subsidized.
- **Smallest viable version.** Run a pilot of `25 cohorts` (`~200 founders`) in one cycle, mixed verticals. Measure completion rate and graduate satisfaction. Scale to the full design if metrics clear targets.
- **Graduate structure.** After graduation, cohorts transition to a "graduate channel" with monthly optional meetings (founder-led, unpaid, optional facilitator support). Persists for as long as the cohort wants it. Some graduate cohorts naturally dissolve after six months. Some persist for years. Both are healthy.

## Risks

**Risk: Open Track participation drops because founders perceive the free program as second-class.** Mitigation: marketing positions the two tracks differently. Open Track is "learn from the YC library." Cohort Track is "ship with a cohort." Different products, not tiers.

**Risk: Cohort Track becomes seen as a back-door to YC core admissions and founders apply for cohort access strategically.** Mitigation: cohort track and core-program admissions are run by separate teams with no information sharing. Cohort participation is a positive signal but not a credential.

**Risk: Facilitator quality varies widely and some cohorts have bad experiences.** Mitigation: facilitator training (two-hour onboarding, ongoing peer feedback among facilitators). Cohort survey after every cycle. Facilitators with consistently low scores are not reassigned.

**Risk: Charging for any part of Startup School breaks the "YC is generous" brand.** Mitigation: the paid track is genuinely opt-in, the Open Track stays free and the paid track's pricing reflects the actual cost of the facilitator and operations infrastructure (not a margin-generating tier).

**Risk: International founders are disadvantaged by time-zone-fragmented cohort meetings.** Mitigation: cohort matching includes time-zone clustering. Asia-Pacific cohorts, Europe cohorts and Americas cohorts run on their own schedules.

## Open questions

Should the Cohort Track replace the existing weak cohort experience in the Open Track, or layer alongside? My read is layer alongside. Keep the open Founder Groups for participants who want them but recognize they are not the program's main value. Communicate clearly that the Cohort Track is the alternative for founders who want depth.

How does this affect the relationship between Startup School and the YC core program? Probably positively. The Cohort Track produces founders who have already practiced shipping under cohort accountability, which is exactly the muscle the core program asks them to use. Conversion from Cohort Track to core-program admit should run higher than conversion from Open Track to core-program admit.

What is the right pricing for the paid track? My instinct is in the `$500 to $1,500` range per founder for twelve weeks. Below `$300` and founders treat it as a frivolous expense. Above `$2,000` and the program excludes founders for whom YC most wants to be accessible. The exact price should be tested in the pilot.

Does this format work for non-software founders (hardware, biotech, climate)? Mostly yes, but the assignment shape needs adjustment. A biotech founder cannot "ship" weekly in the software sense. The weekly artifact for these founders is closer to a research log: customer conversation completed, experiment designed, regulatory step taken. Different artifact, same accountability principle.

How does the Cohort Track interact with vertical-specific YC programming (the YC startup school for biotech, for hard tech, etc.)? The Cohort Track is the generalist program. Vertical-specific deep dives remain separate offerings. The two should be designed to compose, not to compete.
