Andreessen Horowitz opened applications for the Summer 2026 cohort of its Speedrun accelerator on April 28, 2026. Applications close June 8, 2026 at 11:59pm Pacific Time. Speedrun is the firm's games and consumer accelerator, distinct from the broader a16z deal-flow process.

## What it is

Speedrun is a twelve-week in-person accelerator focused on games, consumer apps and interactive entertainment. Each admitted company receives a `$750K` check (terms have varied historically but the standard is an uncapped SAFE with a side letter). The cohort attends programming in San Francisco for the duration, with weekly partner-led sessions, operating-partner workshops and a final cohort showcase.

The Speedrun thesis is that consumer and games founders need a different operating shape than B2B SaaS founders, and the program structures its mentor network, partner engagement and demo format accordingly.

## Key dates and eligibility

- **Applications open.** April 28, 2026.
- **Applications close.** June 8, 2026 at 11:59pm Pacific Time.
- **Decisions.** Communicated by late June.
- **Program start.** Approximately July 14, 2026.
- **Demo / showcase.** Approximately October 4, 2026.
- **Eligibility.** Founders building consumer apps, games, interactive entertainment, content tools or creator economy products. Mix of pre-product and post-product companies accepted. International founders eligible. Willingness to be in San Francisco for the program duration required, with limited exceptions for hardware-bound studios.

## Why it matters now

The cohort composition of Speedrun's first three batches has skewed toward utility and tooling plays rather than content-heavy games, which is structurally a function of the twelve-week format favoring products that can be demonstrated in twelve weeks. By my read, roughly `~70 to 80%` of admitted companies across the first three batches have been utility, tooling, infrastructure or platform plays, with actual content-game studios in the minority.

For S26 specifically, the signal worth tracking is whether the cohort composition shifts. If a16z has internalized the feedback that content-heavy games need longer cycles, the S26 batch may include a more pronounced track for narrative or live-service games. If the cohort composition mirrors the prior batches, the program's design has stabilized around its utility-and-tools center of gravity.

The check size remains `$750K`, which is competitive for the consumer and games stage relative to traditional pre-seed rounds (typically `$500K to $1.5M` in this category). The non-financial value-add is the partner network and the cohort itself, which is the part founders most often cite when comparing Speedrun to alternative paths.

## Open questions

The Speedrun announcement does not explicitly address whether content-heavy games can apply with a vertical-slice-stage product rather than a working demo. Historically the program has accepted content-stage games but the bar has been higher than for tooling plays. Founders in this category should clarify the expected demo state before applying.

The cohort composition for international founders has been roughly `~25 to 35%` in prior batches, which is below YC's biannual cohort international share. Whether the S26 cohort follows the same pattern or shifts toward more international representation is a signal worth tracking but unstated in the announcement.
