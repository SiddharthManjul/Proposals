CATEGORY: EIP
TITLE: Tier a16z Speedrun by IP-density with a twenty-four-week track for content-heavy games

ABSTRACT: a16z Speedrun is a twelve-week accelerator with a `$750K` check, focused on games, consumer and interactive entertainment. The twelve-week shape works well for utility games, casual consumer apps and tooling, but it does not work for content-heavy or IP-density games where the founder needs more time to build a vertical slice that proves the product hypothesis. The wedge is to split Speedrun into two tracks: the existing twelve-week track for utility and infrastructure plays and a new twenty-four-week track for narrative-heavy, IP-density or live-service games. The check size stays the same. The program duration adapts to the product.

BODY:
A games studio in 2026 has two viable shapes for raising a seed round. The first is a utility or social or infrastructure play where the product can be demonstrated as a working prototype in a few weeks: a multiplayer netcode service, a Discord bot for gamers, a content-creation tool for streamers. The second is an actual game with a meaningful content layer, where the bare minimum to assess whether the thing will work is a vertical slice (one playable level, one core mechanic, one art-direction direction) that takes four to six months to build. a16z Speedrun's twelve-week format works for the first shape and structurally disadvantages the second.

## What's wrong with the current format

Speedrun launched in 2024 with the standard accelerator shape: cohort of `~20 companies`, `$750K` check, twelve-week program, demo day at the end. By my read of the first three cohorts, the program is filtering toward utility-and-tooling teams rather than content-heavy teams, because the twelve-week deadline pressures founders into shipping something demoable at the end and demoable-at-twelve-weeks favors utility products.

The actual game studios in the cohort end up using the twelve weeks to ship a "concept demo" or a "teaser trailer" or a pitch deck with concept art, which is not the same artifact as a vertical slice. Investors at demo day see polished marketing materials rather than a playable product. The signal investors are extracting from these companies is "can they make a marketing artifact?" rather than "can they make a game?"

By my count, looking at the cohort composition of the first three Speedrun batches, roughly `~70 to 80%` of admitted companies are utility, tooling, infrastructure or platform plays, with games-as-product representing the minority. This is not what the program's positioning suggests it would be. The selection is not biased against games on purpose. The selection is biased against games because the format makes games hard to evaluate well in twelve weeks.

The cost of this is that a16z is missing the founders building actual games. Those founders need a different commitment shape. They take longer. They need more capital. The twelve-week format selects them out before the program starts because they self-select away.

## Proposed format

Split Speedrun into two tracks running concurrently. The Twelve-Week Track is the existing program. The Twenty-Four-Week Track is new.

**Twelve-Week Track.** Existing shape preserved. Target: utility plays, tooling, infrastructure, casual consumer games where a vertical slice is achievable in twelve weeks. Cohort of `~12 to 15` companies per cycle. `$750K` check unchanged.

**Twenty-Four-Week Track.** New. Target: content-heavy games, narrative-driven titles, live-service plays, games requiring meaningful art and design pipelines. Cohort of `~8 to 12` companies per cycle. Same `$750K` check. Twice the duration with the same capital, meaning the burn rate per company is lower and the founders are expected to be more capital-efficient through the program.

**Cadence.** One cohort per track per six-month cycle. Both tracks run in parallel. Combined demo day at the end of each six-month period, with separate framing for each track ("show us your business" for the twelve-week track, "show us your vertical slice" for the twenty-four-week track).

**Programming differences.** Twelve-week track: weekly product-strategy sessions plus monthly check-ins with operating partners. Twenty-four-week track: weekly product-strategy sessions for the first twelve weeks, then biweekly for the second twelve weeks, plus structured milestones (concept lock at week eight, playable prototype at week sixteen, vertical slice at week twenty-two). The pace of programming matches the pace of product development.

**Host shape.** Same partner team across both tracks. Two a16z games-and-consumer partners as the lead. One operating partner with content-game studio experience for the twenty-four-week track (this is the additional resource the second track requires).

**Success criterion.** Twelve months after each cohort, what fraction raised a Series A from a credible fund? Floor: `~50%` for the twelve-week track, `~40%` for the twenty-four-week track (lower because content games take longer to validate). Stretch: `~70%` and `~60%` respectively. Below those, the format is filtering for the wrong founders.

**Kill criterion.** Two consecutive cohorts on either track with `<25% Series A conversion`. Indicates the track is selecting badly. Re-examine the selection criteria before retiring the track.

## Mechanics

- **Application.** Single application form. Applicants self-identify which track they are applying to, with the option to be considered for both. Selection committee can recommend a different track than the applicant requested.
- **Selection.** Combined committee of a16z partners with track-specific expertise. Roughly `~2,000 to 4,000 applications per cycle`. Admit `~20 to 25 total` across both tracks.
- **Capital structure.** `$750K` check at the same terms (uncapped SAFE or standard accelerator terms). For the twenty-four-week track, the company has roughly twice the runway from the same capital because the program duration is doubled. Founders manage the capital across the longer horizon themselves.
- **Programming budget.** The twelve-week track costs `~$500K to $1M per cycle` in operating expenses (mentors, events, demo day, space). The twenty-four-week track costs `~$700K to $1.2M per cycle` (longer duration, more milestone events). Total annual incremental cost: `~$2M to $4M`.
- **Vertical slice support.** Twenty-four-week track participants get access to a shared art-and-audio contractor pool the program assembles, paid by the program at preferred rates. A founder who needs `~30 hours of contract art direction` to ship the vertical slice can access this without burning their `$750K` on it.
- **Demo day differentiation.** The combined demo day has two formats. Twelve-week track founders give a five-minute pitch with metrics and traction. Twenty-four-week track founders give a ten-minute presentation including a playable demo of the vertical slice (the investors in the room play it live).
- **Smallest viable version.** Run one twenty-four-week track cohort of `~6 to 8` companies first, parallel to the existing twelve-week cycle. Measure outcomes at twelve months. Scale up if the conversion rate meets the floor.

## Risks

**Risk: founders self-select into the longer track to get more runway.** Mitigation: selection committee has final say on track assignment. Founders who could ship in twelve weeks but apply for twenty-four are moved to twelve. Selection criteria for the twenty-four-week track include a clear articulation of why the longer duration is necessary.

**Risk: the twenty-four-week track's lower per-month operating burn (same capital across more months) puts founders in a difficult position if they hit unexpected expenses.** Mitigation: explicit budgeting workshop at week one. Optional bridge financing from a16z at standard terms if a twenty-four-week track company hits unexpected costs.

**Risk: the demo day with playable vertical slices excludes investors who do not have time to play games during demo day.** Mitigation: pre-demo-day access to the vertical slices for invited investors. Playable demos available for two weeks before and two weeks after the formal demo day.

**Risk: the longer track creates "Speedrun fatigue" where founders are in the program past the point of usefulness.** Mitigation: programming intensity drops in the second twelve weeks (biweekly instead of weekly) so founders have space to actually build. The program's role shifts from active programming to milestone gating.

**Risk: differential success rates between tracks create a perception that one track is "better."** Mitigation: track outcomes reported separately and honestly. Different products, different conversion baselines. The comparison that matters is each track's outcomes versus the alternative the founder would have had (no program at all), not versus the other track.

## Open questions

What about hardware-adjacent games (VR/AR, console-exclusive, peripherals)? These often require even longer development cycles than twenty-four weeks plus specialized hardware and supply-chain considerations. The right answer is probably a third track at thirty-six weeks for hardware games, but it should not launch until the twenty-four-week track is proven.

How does the program handle the transition from accelerator to Series A for content games? Content games at the Series A stage typically have an early-access or soft-launch metric, which the program's twenty-four-week timeline does not produce. The program graduates content-game founders into a "post-Speedrun" support cohort with continued operating-partner access for six months after graduation, designed to bridge from vertical-slice to first metrics.

Should both tracks have the same demo day or separate ones? My read is the same demo day with differentiated formats. Investors at the demo day are evaluating both kinds of companies and the comparison helps them calibrate. Splitting the demo day would also fragment a16z's storytelling around the program.

Does the format work for non-games consumer plays (social apps, creator tools, dating apps)? Yes, with some adaptation. Most non-games consumer plays fit the twelve-week track. The exception is content-heavy social apps (specific creator categories where the founder is also producing content as proof) where the twenty-four-week shape might fit. Selection committee can route case-by-case.

How does this format compose with a16z's other consumer-and-games activities (the Cultural Leadership Fund, the Games Fund proper, the Crypto Fund for game-economy plays)? The accelerator is the front-funnel. The dedicated funds are the follow-on financing. The composition should be more explicit in Speedrun's program design: "graduating into a16z Games Fund follow-on" should be a published possible outcome.
