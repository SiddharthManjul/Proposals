Lightspeed Venture Partners opened applications for the 2026 cohort of its Generation fellowship on May 12, 2026. Applications close June 30, 2026. Generation is a year-long fellowship for student builders aspiring to start companies, with cohort programming, partner access and a modest stipend.

## What it is

Lightspeed Generation is a fellowship for current undergraduate and graduate students who are building or planning to build technology companies. The 2026 cohort admits roughly `~30 to 50` students for the academic year, with structured programming throughout, partner office hours, a network of operating partners and Lightspeed portfolio CEOs as mentors, and an annual fellowship convening event.

The fellowship is non-investment (no equity is taken from fellows or any companies they form during the program), but admitted fellows have a structured relationship with Lightspeed for future investment conversations if they choose to start companies during or after their studies.

## Key dates and eligibility

- **Applications open.** May 12, 2026.
- **Applications close.** June 30, 2026.
- **Decisions.** Communicated by August 1, 2026.
- **Program start.** Approximately September 8, 2026.
- **Program completion.** Approximately August 30, 2027.
- **Stipend.** Approximately `$5,000 to $10,000` per fellow for the academic year, plus travel reimbursement for in-person events.
- **Eligibility.** Currently enrolled undergraduate or graduate students at any accredited institution. International students eligible. Strong preference for students with demonstrated building experience (a prior project, an open-source contribution, a research paper, a previous startup attempt) over students who have not yet shipped anything. No vertical or stage restrictions.

## Why it matters now

The student fellowship category has grown crowded in recent years, with Lightspeed Generation now competing for the same talent pool as Z Fellows, the South Park Commons Fellowship, the Thiel Fellowship (different structure but adjacent population) and several university-affiliated programs. By my read of the publicly visible outcomes data across these programs, conversion from student fellowship to actually-funded-startup-within-three-years sits in the `~20 to 35%` range, comparable to the baseline rate for similarly-credentialed students who did not attend any fellowship.

The signal that distinguishes Generation from peer programs is the Lightspeed partner attention and the portfolio mentor network, which can be materially valuable to students who specifically want to build in categories where Lightspeed has portfolio depth (enterprise SaaS, consumer, AI infrastructure, marketplaces, healthcare and selected deep-tech verticals).

For 2026 specifically, the cohort design appears unchanged from prior years (centralized programming with the center of gravity in San Francisco, supplemented by virtual sessions for non-Bay-Area fellows). Whether Lightspeed is considering geographic decentralization of the fellowship (regional cohorts in Boston, NYC, Bangalore or Cambridge UK) is not addressed in the announcement.

## Open questions

The application process for Generation has historically included a written application, a video introduction and at least one interview round. The 2026 announcement does not specify whether the format has changed. Students should expect a multi-round process spanning approximately four to six weeks from application close to decision.

The stipend amount has shifted across cohorts, with some prior fellows reporting stipends below the publicly stated range. Whether the stipend is consistent across all admitted fellows or varies by location, financial-aid status or program track is not specified in the announcement.
