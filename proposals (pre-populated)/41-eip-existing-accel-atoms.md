CATEGORY: EIP
TITLE: Add a six-month global bridge cohort to Accel Atoms for India founders with international ambitions

ABSTRACT: Accel Atoms is Accel India's pre-seed accelerator with a strong cohort, a tight India-first product orientation and excellent local mentor density. The program works exceptionally for India-market companies. It does not provide an obvious next step for the meaningful subset of Atoms founders whose product is structurally global from inception (devtools, AI infrastructure, vertical SaaS for non-India buyers, B2B software with cross-border distribution). The wedge is to add a six-month "Atoms Global" bridge cohort, layered on top of the existing Atoms program, focused specifically on bridging India-built companies to international customers, hiring, and capital. Atoms remains the foundation. The bridge cohort is the optional second act.

BODY:
The India startup ecosystem in 2026 has solved most of the basic problems it had a decade ago. There is local capital. There is product-engineering talent at scale. There are local accelerators run by global brands. The remaining hard problem for the most ambitious India founders is not getting to product-market fit in India. It is getting from India product-market fit to global market presence without rebuilding the company twice. The path is well-documented for a few exit-stage success stories and almost entirely uncharted for the seed and Series A founders who are walking it now.

## What's wrong with the current format

Accel Atoms runs as a structured pre-seed accelerator with a cohort of `~15 to 20 companies` per cycle, two cycles per year. The program is `~6 months` long, includes meaningful capital (`~$250K to $500K` checks) and provides strong programming on India-market product-market fit, distribution and team building. The cohort quality is high and the brand is strong.

By my read of the cohort outcomes data and conversations with Atoms alumni, the program delivers what it advertises for India-first companies. The structural gap shows up in a specific subset of the cohort: companies whose products are global from inception (devtools, AI infrastructure, vertical SaaS sold to non-India buyers, B2B software with cross-border distribution). These companies, roughly `~30 to 45%` of any given Atoms cohort by my count, face a different post-program transition than their India-market peers.

The India-market companies have a clear next step: raise a Series A from India or India-active funds, scale local distribution, hire from the India talent pool. The global-product companies have a harder transition: their next customers are in the US or Europe, their next Series A is most likely from a US fund that has limited context on India-based founders, and their next hires need to span India engineering and US-or-Europe-based go-to-market. The program does not currently structure for this transition because the program's design assumes India market continuity.

The cost of the gap is observable in the outcomes data. Global-product Atoms alumni take roughly `~50 to 80%` longer to close their Series A compared to India-market Atoms alumni at similar stages of revenue. They also frequently relocate the company or a founding team member to the US or Europe at significant cost during this transition, with the relocation often happening at the worst possible time (during the active Series A fundraising cycle).

Accel has the geographic footprint to fix this structurally. Accel is one of the rare global VC platforms with deep partner presence in both India and the US/Europe. The Atoms program could be the bridge that uses both halves of the firm.

## Proposed format

Add an "Atoms Global" cohort that runs as a six-month bridge program after the standard Atoms cycle ends. Open only to Atoms graduates whose products are structurally global. Layered on top of Atoms, not a replacement for it.

**Eligibility.** Atoms graduates from the prior twelve months. Self-selected for global-product orientation, with selection committee review. Roughly `~5 to 8 companies` per cohort, drawn from one to two prior Atoms batches.

**Duration.** Six months. Three months structured programming, three months structured follow-through with reduced cadence.

**Cadence.** Two cohorts per year. Each cohort overlaps with the next standard Atoms cycle but operates separately.

**Programming structure.** Months one and two: US-and-Europe customer discovery and pipeline development, with structured introductions to relevant Accel portfolio companies and the firm's US and European partners. Month three: in-person two-week intensive in the US (San Francisco or NYC), with daily customer calls, partner working sessions and a structured Series A fundraising rehearsal. Months four to six: founder-managed, with biweekly check-ins with the Atoms Global lead partner and ad-hoc working sessions when needed.

**Capital.** No new check at the Atoms Global stage. The original Atoms check already provided runway. The bridge cohort provides structure and network, not capital, except in cases where bridge financing makes sense (these are decided case by case, similar to Atoms-to-Series-A bridges generally).

**Host shape.** A dedicated Atoms Global lead partner with active relationships in both India and the US. Likely a partner who has previously moved between Accel India and Accel US, or a partner with deep cross-border deal experience. Plus support from US-based Accel partners during the in-person intensive.

**Success criterion.** Twelve months after each cohort, the percentage of bridge-cohort companies that have closed a Series A from a US or European fund (Accel or other). Floor: `~50%`. Stretch: `~70%`. Plus median time-to-Series-A-close: floor `~9 months`, stretch `~6 months`. Below the floor, the program is not bridging the gap it was designed for.

**Kill criterion.** Two consecutive cohorts with Series A conversion below `~30%` or median time-to-Series-A-close above `~14 months`. Indicates the bridge is not delivering its promised acceleration.

## Mechanics

- **Selection.** Eligibility opens to Atoms graduates within twelve months of program completion. Companies self-apply with a written articulation of their global product thesis and target customer geography. Selection committee includes both Atoms (India) partners and US Accel partners, jointly deciding.
- **In-person intensive.** Two weeks in the US (SF or NYC). Accel covers travel and accommodation for one founder per company. Accommodation in shared founder housing rather than hotels, to encourage intra-cohort relationship density.
- **Customer-pipeline support.** During months one to two, the Atoms Global team makes structured introductions to relevant Accel portfolio companies in the US and Europe. The introduction quality is the program's specific value-add and depends on the lead partner's relationship density.
- **Fundraising preparation.** During the in-person intensive, the program structures a Series A fundraising rehearsal with US partners as the audience. Honest feedback. No deal commitment in the room. The rehearsal compresses what would otherwise be eight to twelve weeks of unstructured pitch refinement.
- **Budget.** Travel and intensive cost: `~$80K to $150K per cohort` (`~$15K to $25K per company`). Lead partner time: `~25% of one partner's annual time`. Plus operational coordination. Total annual incremental cost: `~$500K to $800K` for two cohorts per year.
- **Documentation.** Internal one-page summary per company per quarter, tracking pipeline-quality, customer-conversation count, fundraising progress. Not externally published.
- **Smallest viable version.** Run a pilot cohort of three to four companies first. Measure Series A conversion and time-to-close versus a control group of Atoms graduates who did not enter the bridge. If the bridge cohort outperforms by a meaningful margin, scale to full size.

## Risks

**Risk: the bridge becomes a "second-tier" credential for Atoms graduates and creates two classes of alumni.** Mitigation: explicit framing as an opt-in additional program for a specific founder profile, not as a hierarchy. Founders whose products are India-first do not need the bridge and are not disadvantaged by not joining it. The program structure should communicate this clearly.

**Risk: the in-person intensive in the US disadvantages founders with visa or family constraints that limit US travel.** Mitigation: visa-support and accelerated travel logistics handled by the program team. For founders who genuinely cannot travel to the US, run a parallel virtual-intensive option, with the understanding that the in-person version produces higher network density.

**Risk: Series A conversion data is hard to attribute cleanly to the bridge cohort versus general Atoms outcomes.** Mitigation: track cohort-vs-non-cohort outcomes carefully across multiple cycles. Accept some attribution uncertainty in the first two years. By year three, the data should support clearer comparisons.

**Risk: the bridge cohort pulls Accel India partners' attention away from new Atoms cohorts.** Mitigation: dedicated bridge-cohort lead partner. India Atoms partners' time on the bridge cohort is limited (mostly the selection decision plus optional advisory).

**Risk: the program is perceived as Accel pushing its India founders to abandon India and go global.** Mitigation: framing emphasizes that the bridge is for companies that were always going to be global, not for companies that would have stayed India-focused. The India-market path for India-market companies is supported separately and not relegated.

## Open questions

What about Atoms graduates more than twelve months out who decide later that they want global expansion? Probably open the program to them on a case-by-case basis. The twelve-month window is for the typical case. The bridge cohort is fundamentally useful for any India-built company at an inflection point regardless of when they entered Atoms.

How does this format interact with the broader Accel global program (Accel UK, Accel Israel, Accel US)? The bridge cohort uses all of them. The customer-pipeline introductions, the partner working sessions and the fundraising rehearsal all draw on the global firm's network. The bridge is the operational concentration of what is otherwise distributed across multiple Accel teams.

Should the bridge cohort be expanded to non-Atoms India founders (founders Accel India did not originally invest in but who fit the bridge profile)? Probably yes, in year three or four after the format is proven on Atoms graduates. The Atoms-only constraint exists to keep the cohort tight in the first generations.

What about founders whose product is global but who do not want to relocate or eventually relocate to the US? The bridge program does not require relocation. The two-week intensive is a network-building event, not a relocation event. Companies can run cross-border with engineering in India and go-to-market in the US, and the program supports both shapes.

How does this affect Accel's competitive position versus other global VCs trying to compete for the same India-built-global-targeting founders? Strongly positively. No other major VC has both the India accelerator footprint and the US/Europe partner depth at Accel's scale. The bridge cohort is a structural advantage that competitors would have to build a multi-year cross-border partner organization to match.
